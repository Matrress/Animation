  1. Seven-zone responsive support

    Seven zones run under the body: head, shoulder, back, pelvis, knees, legs and ankles. Each one carries a different share of your weight, so the heavy pelvis is held firmly while the lighter head and shoulders settle in. The bars show the support each zone gives.

  2. Shoulder adaptation

    The shoulder is the widest part of a side sleeper. The latex yields here, so the shoulder settles into the surface instead of being pushed back, and the spine can stay level.

  3. Lumbar support

    Under the waist the latex rises to meet the natural curve of the lower back. It supports the hollow without pushing into it, so the spine keeps its neutral line.

  4. Reinforced pelvic stability

    The pelvis carries the most weight. This zone is reinforced, so the hips do not sink below the shoulders and the spine is not bent out of line.

  5. Stretching effect

    When the spine rests in a neutral line, the muscles around it can let go and the spine lengthens gently into its natural shape. The drawing shows the principle. It is not a medical measurement.

  6. Soft-tissue and muscle relief

    Latex is elastic. It gives under muscle and soft tissue and comes back, following your shape without holding it, so muscles are not working to push against the surface.

  7. Pressure distribution and circulation comfort

    Latex spreads your weight over a wider area, so no single point carries too much. Lower, more even pressure helps you stay comfortable and change position freely. A mattress supports comfort. It does not treat medical conditions, so if you have one, ask your doctor.

  8. Real natural latex response

    This is the real thing: a person on natural Dunlop latex. Open cells and high elasticity let it press in under load and return straight away, which is why it can support and adapt at the same time.
