Not too hard. Not too soft. Adapted and supported.

Lying on your side, your shoulder and hip must sink in while your waist is carried. Only then does the spine stay straight.

Too firm

The body rests on the shoulder and the hip only, so the support is concentrated, not distributed.

  • 1Shoulder overloaded. All the weight lands on one point.
  • 2Waist and pelvis not supported. The spine arches over an empty gap.
  • 3Calves lose contact. The surface does not adapt, so circulation is not helped.

Too soft

The pelvis sinks further than the legs and the body hangs in a gentle reverse arch. The anatomical balance is lost.

  • 1Pelvis sinks too far. It carries more than the legs and drags the body down.
  • 2Vertebrae out of line. The spine is no longer straight, it follows the hollow.

Correct: adapted and supported

Shoulder and hip sink exactly as far as they need, the waist is carried and the seven vertebrae sit in one straight line. This is the balance Dunlop regulation looks for, the one in the model above.

  • 1Shoulder sinks in and is relieved.
  • 2Waist and pelvis carried, no gap.
  • 3Calves supported, circulation is free.
