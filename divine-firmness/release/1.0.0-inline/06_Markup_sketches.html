Not too hard. Not too soft. Adapted and supported.

Lying on your side, your shoulder and hip must sink in while your waist is carried. Only then does the spine stay straight.

Too firm

The body rests on the shoulder, so the support is concentrated, not distributed. The middle arches up and the spine arches with it.

  • 1Shoulder overloaded. All the weight lands on one point.
  • 2Waist and pelvis not supported. The spine arches up over an empty gap.
  • 3Calves lose contact. The surface does not adapt, so circulation is not helped.
  • 4Spine arched up. The vertebrae leave their line and the spine is under strain.

Too soft

The pelvis sinks further than the legs and the body hangs in a gentle reverse arch. The spine bends down with it and the anatomical balance is lost.

  • 1Pelvis sinks too far. It carries more than the legs and drags the body down.
  • 2Spine sagging down. The vertebrae are out of line, the spine follows the hollow.

Correct: adapted and supported

Shoulder and hip sink exactly as far as they need, the waist is carried and the seven vertebrae sit in one straight line. This is the balance Dunlop regulation looks for, the one in the model above.

  • 1Shoulder sinks in and is relieved. The pillow keeps the head level.
  • 2Waist and pelvis carried, no gap.
  • 3Calves supported, circulation is free.
  • 4Spine straight, seven vertebrae in one line.

Sit or lie: where a latex mattress is meant to work

A latex mattress is made for lying down. Sitting on it, or bouncing on it, tells you nothing about how it carries your sleep.

Sitting: all your weight on one small spot

Seated, most of your weight rests on the small area under your pelvis. The same weight on a much smaller area means much higher pressure, so the surface gives way and you sink in. This is normal, and it says nothing about how the mattress carries you in sleep.

  • 1Small area, high pressure. Most of your weight is gathered under the pelvis.
  • 2The latex gives way. The surface sinks much deeper than it ever does when you sleep.
  • 3Do not judge it by sitting. A good latex mattress is not meant to feel springy under a seated body.

Lying: weight spread, the latex carries you

Lying down, the same weight is spread over the whole length of your body, from head to ankles. The open cells of the latex answer each zone in proportion, so you rest on top of the surface, not in it. This is where a latex mattress does its job, and where its resilience belongs.

  • 1Weight spread over all seven zones, not one spot.
  • 2The latex answers each zone and pushes back evenly.
  • 3Held on the surface, with the spine in one line.
