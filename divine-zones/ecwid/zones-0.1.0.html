Engineering view

Seven zones, each one supporting the body in its own way

Seven zones run under the body: head, shoulder, back, pelvis, knees, legs and ankles. Each one carries a different share of your weight, so every part of the body gets the support that suits it. The bars show how support is distributed between the zones.

1Head 2Shoulder 3Back 4Pelvis 5Knees 6Legs 7Ankles
  1. 1

    Head

    Settles in

    The head is one of the lighter parts of the body. This zone gives way and lets the head settle in, instead of pushing it back.

  2. 2

    Shoulder

    Yields

    The shoulder is the widest part of a side sleeper and takes a large share of the load. The latex yields here, so the shoulder settles into the surface and the spine can stay level.

  3. 3

    Back

    Rises to meet

    Under the waist the latex rises to meet the natural curve of the lower back. It supports the hollow without pushing into it, so the spine keeps its neutral line.

  4. 4

    Pelvis

    Holds firm

    The pelvis carries the most weight. This zone is reinforced, so the hips do not sink below the shoulders and the spine is not bent out of line.

  5. 5

    Knees

    Spreads the load

    Latex spreads the weight of the knees over a wider area, so no single point carries too much and the joint is not pressed into the surface.

  6. 6

    Legs

    Follows the shape

    Latex is elastic. It gives under muscle and soft tissue and comes back, following your shape without holding it, so muscles do not work to push against the surface.

  7. 7

    Ankles

    Releases

    At the far end the load is light. Lower, more even pressure here helps you stay comfortable and change position freely.

  8. The engineering principle

    One body, seven different loads. A single setting cannot suit all of them, so support is divided into zones and each zone answers its own part of the body.

    The heavy pelvis is held firmly while the lighter head and shoulders settle in. This is a principle of load distribution, not a medical measurement.
